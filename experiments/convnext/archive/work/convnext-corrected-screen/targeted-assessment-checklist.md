# Targeted-data result assessment

This checklist was written before the targeted and fresh-discriminator final results. It evaluates the data intervention as a package: a changed source mixture plus selected 190 ms discriminator views. It cannot isolate those two factors.

1. **Establish the comparison.** Require identical step-8,090 student and optimizer state, 400 successful generator updates, the same per-position valid sample counts, an identical held-out panel, and passed CPU stream checks. Compare targeted against regular, then independently against step 8,090. Do not substitute the previous nominal broad-emotion mixture for the measured control: regular is 99.630% speech; targeted has 5.077% explicitly labeled expressive/whisper material.
2. **Check ordinary speech before declaring a winner.** Use paired sample-weighted waveform MAE, the unchanged diagnostic mel metric, and nonquiet correlation together. Inspect affected language/source groups and worst crops so an aggregate gain cannot hide a language or speaker failure. All 22 Indic languages in training is coverage evidence, not a quality guarantee for every language. Do not treat tiny numerical differences as audible improvements or equivalence.
3. **Check the intended expressive cases.** Separate whistle, scream, laugh, breath, and whisper outcomes where source labels exist. Report distinct held-out source counts beside apparent gains. Correlation alone is insufficient; also inspect waveform error, mel error, high-frequency residual, and peak excursions. The new Yell example is a single diagnostic. There is no separate reviewed held-out crying, giggling, or shouting cohort, so those gains cannot be asserted.
4. **Check natural quiet independently from synthetic fixtures.** Prefer pooled RMS on the fixed teacher-quiet mask, with failed-window counts, DC/phase-periodic structure, and both the encoded-zero and quiet-noise fixtures. Keep startup and mature output distinct where diagnostics permit. Lower quiet RMS is encouraging, but all windows still failing would mean the silence problem remains unresolved. The training selector measured input quiet, not teacher-output quiet.
5. **Check overshoot severity and breadth.** Use maximum peak plus affected source/crop counts, not only the number of samples exceeding one. Overlapping evaluation crops can count the same physical event repeatedly. A reduced peak with more affected sources is a mixed result, not an automatic win.
6. **Check concentration and generalization limits.** Report whether improvements come from a few sources or are broadly paired. Source bootstrap intervals describe held-out source sampling only; they omit training-seed uncertainty and do not prove perceptual equivalence. The 1,096.62 seconds of parent-exposed event data are an authorized debugging reuse, not held-out leakage, but cannot be described as entirely unseen training exposure.
7. **Decide narrowly.** Keep targeted sampling provisionally only if it gives a useful improvement in intended cases without a meaningful speech, quiet, or peak regression. If quiet improves over the regular control but remains worse than step 8,090, describe it as mitigation of continuation drift. If gains and regressions trade off, retain the checkpoint and mark the package unresolved rather than call it quality preserving. Assess calibrated magnitude/complex additions separately using their matched controls; do not attribute their gains to sampling alone.

The regular-only preview already demonstrates the need for two baselines. Against step 8,090, natural waveform MAE improved 0.955% and nonquiet correlation increased 0.00278, while fixed diagnostic mel worsened 0.658% and natural quiet pooled RMS worsened 24.865%. These are preview observations, not conclusions about the still-pending targeted result.
