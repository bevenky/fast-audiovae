These gains include the previous candidate's progress; they must not all be attributed to the new L1 experiment.

**What the loss-balance correction established.** All nine calibration batches had finite, nonzero L1/mel parameter gradients. The median produced a fixed mel coefficient of 0.001979136889896455 with waveform coefficient one. AdamW at 1e-6 passed on the first trial, without rate reductions. Averaged across the nine disposable updates on the same complete calibration panel, both predicted and actual waveform/mel loss changes were favorable.

Raw L1 allocates 13.96% of waveform-output gradient energy to quiet samples on this 72-source panel. This removes the prior repair objectives' extreme quiet amplification or starvation. It does not make every parameter group or optimizer step equally influenced: the pooled-panel weighted mel gradient remains 2.50 times waveform initially and 3.07 times at the endpoint. Median per-batch norm calibration is not pooled-gradient equality.

Approximately 95% of mel parameter-gradient energy concentrates in the head convolution bias, while waveform energy is distributed differently, predominantly through output weights. That is a useful observation, not proof that the bias causes every failure. AdamW's actual coordinate updates matter too. The first ordinary training-batch probe worsens broader-panel mel while improving its own batch; the early and final probes improve both broader-panel losses. They use different update batches and moment states, so they do not isolate a time or optimizer effect.

Natural quiet RMS also worsens on the training calibration panel by about 0.34%, even though its quiet mel improves. Thus the remaining issue is not solely held-out generalization. Average absolute waveform error, squared quiet residual error, spectral detail and peak limits are distinct objectives. Correcting their numerical scales does not guarantee simultaneous improvement in every region.

**Recommendation.** Stop this bounded comparison here and preserve its evidence. Your alternative is a sensible next architecture investigation: inherit AudioVAE2's trained decoder, then reduce coupled channel widths in the expensive middle stages. Retain the final convolution and tanh initially. Corresponding stages have aligned temporal grids, making teacher guidance easier, but pruned widths require explicitly selected channels or training-only projections. Final recovery must run the complete student chain; independently matching blocks on teacher inputs is insufficient.

The archived static count places 71.27% of decoder MACs in the 1.2, 6 and 12 kHz stages, with the 6 kHz stage largest. This points to focused structured pruning rather than deleting arbitrary layers or merely zeroing weights. It is a lower-risk starting representation, not a promise of Supertonic-like RTF or unchanged perceptual quality. The detailed fallback note explains the inherited-weight and dependency checks.
