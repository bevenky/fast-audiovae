**Recommendation: correct the recent repair objective and its calibration before changing decoder layers.** The comparison with AudioVAE2 and Supertonic supports keeping the existing inexpensive head for that controlled comparison. It does not establish that balancing alone will fix the older training plateau, natural quiet reconstruction or peak overshoot.

This audit, dated September 10, 2026, inspected saved measurements, our training code, the frozen teacher source and current primary upstream sources. No model inference, training, benchmark, checkpoint promotion or commit was performed. The retained joint-head candidate and original step 8,890 checkpoint are unchanged.

**The measured imbalance belongs to the recent pilots.**

The full training recipe already used raw, sample-pooled teacher waveform L1, multiresolution mel, output-gradient balancing, adversarial loss and feature matching. The recent head-repair experiments used different waveform objectives and omitted GAN updates for isolation. These distinctions matter when interpreting their failures.

| Objective in the recent experiments | Saved evidence | What follows |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Separately normalized quiet/active MSE | The quiet denominator was about 15,802 times smaller. Initial quiet output-gradient norm was about 1,016 times active on the fixed probe. | Similar numerical loss values hid very different influences. Retire inverse-regional-error scaling from the next diagnostic. |
| Globally pooled MSE | Quiet provided only 0.00439% of calibration waveform-gradient energy. Waveform parameter-gradient norm was 11.6–15.5 times mel despite output-level calibration. | Pooling alone moved to the opposite extreme. It improved MAE but worsened stationary noise by 16.6% and average mel by 0.62%. |
| Existing raw waveform L1 | Every nonzero valid sample error has the same derivative magnitude before other losses and balancing. | This avoids amplitude-driven squaring and division by tiny regional errors. It is the simplest existing reconstruction contract to restore. |

With the fitting pool's sample counts and nonzero errors, L1 would allocate about 13.31% of waveform-output gradient energy to quiet samples. That is an analytical consequence of the sample counts, not a newly measured result or a guarantee about parameter updates. L1 also does not enforce a hard peak bound or automatically eliminate small-error oscillation.

The original EnCodec-style balancer is not incorrectly implemented merely because parameter contributions differ. It balances gradients at generated audio; the decoder's derivatives and optimizer subsequently transform them. A negative waveform/mel gradient cosine is also insufficient to prove destructive training: in one saved regional-MSE probe their sum still locally descends both losses. In the pooled-MSE probe the sum instead locally worsens mel under infinitesimal plain SGD. Neither calculation reconstructs every actual AdamW step. **The next bounded comparison should have this precise contract.**

1. Start a disposable copy of the retained joint-head candidate. Keep the exact frozen 64-channel encoder means and teacher waveforms, calibrated normalization, causal context, full-head trainable scope and current long-mel definition. Start calibration with the preceding head pilot's fresh AdamW at 1e-6; its actual-update check must establish whether that rate remains suitable for the new objective. Any rate change must be recorded and matched in the eventual control. No new layer, optimizer or inference operation enters this comparison.
2. Use teacher waveform L1 across every valid sample, with quiet per-sample weight one. Remove the old-student preservation target and both regional and global MSE normalizations. Keep sample/frame pooling and exclude context and padded tails exactly as before. Selection should protect teacher-relative quality, rather than require near-identity to the old student's errors.
3. Derive one fixed mel coefficient on a representative training-only calibration panel. A defensible starting value is the median of the per-batch ratio of L1 and mel gradient norms over **all trainable head parameters**. Report individual head layers too. Skip and disclose genuinely zero-gradient cases instead of creating enormous coefficients through division by epsilon. This is an initialization heuristic, not a guarantee or a new adaptive optimizer.
4. Verify the actual AdamW displacement before accepting that coefficient. For each loss gradient g and actual weight change delta, g dot delta predicts the local loss change. Check the actual same-panel change as well. Include the real clipping, moments, second moments and decay, restoring every disposable trial's complete state. Repeat these observations early and at the end of the eventual bounded pilot because fresh Adam moments do not describe later updates. Equal gradient norms alone are insufficient.
5. If a tradeoff remains, classify it before acting: a bad predicted direction implicates weighting/optimizer geometry; a favorable prediction but bad finite change implicates step size or curvature; improvement on fitting probes with held-out regressions implicates coverage/generalization or differing objectives. Do not solve all three with arbitrary loss-weight changes. Do not demand monotonic improvement on every noisy individual update.
6. Keep the original full RecipeV2 EMA balancer untouched during this head diagnostic. If a later full continuation is justified, restore its actual optimizer/discriminator states and scheduled GAN/FM contributions. Do not reinstate a 0.99 correlation requirement before allowing perceptual training. The numerical coefficient cannot responsibly be specified until this L1 objective and trainable scope have been measured.

This changes the recent diagnostic's objective and calibration, not the deployed graph. It does **not** establish an RTF improvement. **AudioVAE2 and Supertonic solve related but different rendering problems.**

The teacher progressively synthesizes audio through residual upsampling stages, then a waveform-rate convolution and tanh. Supertonic's released decoder performs most learned work at a low frame rate, then uses a nonlinear head to emit waveform blocks. Our student already has the ten ConvNeXt blocks, residual paths and nonlinear direct-output head. There is no missing Snake layer in Supertonic 3: its blocks use GELU and its head uses PReLU. The inspected graph has no final tanh, clipping, sample-rate postfilter or ConvNeXt V2 GRN. [Teacher implementation](https://github.com/OpenBMB/VoxCPM/blob/main/src/voxcpm/modules/audiovae/audio_vae_v2.py), [released Supertonic graph](https://huggingface.co/Supertone/supertonic-3/blob/main/onnx/vocoder.onnx).

| Remaining issue | What AudioVAE2 does | What Supertonic establishes | Consequence for this student |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Repeating stationary silence | Our paired trace shows learned cancellation in its final multichannel convolution. Tanh leaves the tiny waveform essentially unchanged. | Direct block output also requires a learned mapping for silence. No special decoder silence gate was found. | Joint head adaptation already reduced this residual by 49.2%. Existing features support a readout that approximates the stationary target very closely. Correct their training before assuming a capacity limit. |
| Natural quiet, breathing and transitions | Reconstructs a small, changing waveform, not necessarily zeros. | Application-inserted zero gaps do not establish natural quiet reconstruction. | A fixed silence template or mute gate cannot repair the input-dependent residual. Preserve teacher-aligned waveform and local spectral detail. |
| Loud sample overshoot | Terminal tanh provides a hard range bound; measured teacher pre-tanh peaks near 2.97 become about 0.995. | The raw released graph is unbounded. Its browser WAV writer clamps samples outside the graph. | Teacher targets encourage correct amplitude but cannot guarantee bounded output from an unbounded head. Treat a true bound as a separate requirement with an adaptation and CPU cost. |
| Phase and rapid changes | Progressive high-rate residual processing and a final convolution explicitly couple nearby waveform samples. | The temporal body and nonlinear frame head learn waveform blocks; perceptual losses judge the resulting audio. | A different synthesis bias, not a proven missing layer. Our short-spectral and auxiliary-feature pilots had mixed results; do not stack them as established fixes. |
| Conditioning compatibility | Its latent representation was learned for its original synthesis network. | The published recipe trains an acoustic encoder and decoder together; the exact V3 acoustic frontend/trainer is not released in inspected assets. | We intentionally freeze someone else's latent representation. Preserving all 64 coordinates does not guarantee that the new decoder can use them equally easily. |

The stationary trace is particularly informative. Almost constant student head features pass through 480 different sample-position projections, creating a repeating 10 ms waveform. At the original 8,890 checkpoint, 93.19% of stationary residual power had this pattern. The teacher also develops phase structure internally, then suppresses it at its learned output. Thus phase structure itself is not a bug unique to the student. A successful stationary readout does not prove that the same correction preserves other audio. **We cannot assume Supertonic has already solved our exact edge cases.**

The published SupertonicTTS recipe trains its own acoustic representation and uses mel, adversarial and feature-matching supervision. Its reported reconstruction/perceptual evaluations do not establish sample-aligned AudioVAE2 reconstruction, our natural-silence limits or raw-float peak behavior. The current V3 configuration differs from the paper's acoustic frontend, so paper details must not be promoted to a verified complete V3 training recipe. [SupertonicTTS paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.23108v3), [V3 configuration](https://huggingface.co/Supertone/supertonic-3/blob/main/onnx/tts.json).

The official browser writer explicitly clamps samples to [-1,1] before PCM16 conversion; the Python decoder path returns raw floats. The high-level pipeline also inserts exact-zero gaps between text chunks. These are output/application behaviors, not evidence that the neural decoder itself avoids overshoots or reconstructs quiet codec input correctly. This audit did not measure Supertonic overshoots, and does not claim its quality comes from clipping. [Browser writer](https://github.com/supertone-oss-archive/supertonic/blob/main/web/helper.js#L522-L526), [raw return](https://github.com/supertone-oss-archive/supertonic-py/blob/main/supertonic/core.py#L481-L482), [inserted silence](https://github.com/supertone-oss-archive/supertonic-py/blob/main/supertonic/pipeline.py#L277-L298).

Our body also runs at 100 internal frames/second, versus approximately 86.13 for the released Supertonic decoder: ours processes about 16.1% more frames per audio second. This is an arithmetic rate comparison, not a measured end-to-end runtime penalty. Its latent width, head dimensions, runtime and batching also differ. Equal block counts do not promise equal RTF.

**Additional gaps worth fixing are in coverage and measurement, not another unverified layer.**

- The initial 32-source calibration panel included laughter and whispering but lacked the other difficult expressive categories. Source labels alone do not verify that a fitting crop, or its randomly selected 190 ms discriminator view, contains the event. Use verified training event intervals, preserving causal history, before claiming expressive calibration coverage. Selection currently lacks verified crying, human-whistling and shouting sources; the repeatedly inspected development panel is not an unseen final test.
- The automatic spectral gates cover whole-source results. Quiet, active and transition measurements have been separate diagnostics. Declare their role and minimum support before another trial; do not let an active-speech average hide quiet regressions or treat one sparsely supported FFT window as a whole event.
- A log-mel floor suppresses that branch's gradient below the floor. Linear mel and waveform L1 still provide supervision. Saved evidence does not establish that floor saturation causes current silence failures, so changing the floor now would be speculative. Magnitude spectra also do not uniquely specify phase.
- Neither GAN/FM nor complex spectral discriminators provide a universal amplitude guarantee. DAC's actual discriminator preprocessing removes DC and peak-normalizes each input, which limits its ability to enforce absolute noise level or output ceiling. That is DAC evidence, not a verified V2 discriminator setting, and our base discriminator does not use that amplitude normalization. [DAC preprocessing](https://github.com/descriptinc/descript-audio-codec/blob/main/dac/model/discriminator.py#L193-L202).
- AudioVAE2's full decoder-pretraining hyperparameters were not disclosed in the inspected V2 report. Its TTS flow-matching optimizer settings are not its codec recipe. The original AudioVAE paper documents mel/adversarial/KL training, but a KL term cannot change a frozen encoder posterior in our decoder-only experiment. [V2 report](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.06928#S3.SS2), [original AudioVAE training](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.24650#S3.SS5).

Weight normalization gave no useful incremental gain in the completed head pilot. Short-window supervision, last-block adaptation and auxiliary teacher features gave mixed results and failed selection. None should be described as a confirmed missing AudioVAE2 ingredient whose addition now guarantees improvement. The retained candidate remains the common reference; no combination is justified yet.

The immediate decision is one controlled L1-plus-mel head comparison with parameter and actual-update calibration, after closing the calibration coverage gap. Keep peak bounding separate. If that comparison still trades quiet against active reconstruction, its update-level evidence will determine whether to change optimization, data coverage or the representation. There is presently evidence of a correctable experimental objective problem, but no proof that this inexpensive architecture can or cannot reach the final teacher-fidelity goal.
